## 1. Required disclosures

Amazon requires **two layers**. FTC requires the material-connection layer. The Amazon identification sentence does **not** replace the FTC placement rule, and a `/disclosure` page does **not** replace either.

### 1.1 Amazon identification sentence (mandatory wording)

Operating Agreement **§5 Identifying Yourself as an Associate** (Oct 15, 2025):

> You must clearly and prominently state the following, or any substantially similar statement previously allowed under this Agreement, on your Site or any other location where Amazon may authorize your display or other use of Program Content: **“As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.”**

Use that sentence **verbatim**. Do not swap in a paraphrase on the theory that it is “substantially similar.” The “substantially similar” clause only grandfathered older Amazon-approved wording. Amazon’s own help page repeats the same sentence and says it must “clearly and conspicuously appear[] on your Site.”

**Exact phrase to put on the site (copy-paste):**

```
As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.
```

Also from OA §5 (do not violate):

> Except for this disclosure, and other than as required by applicable law, you will not make any public communication with respect to this Agreement or your participation in the Associates Program without our advance written permission. You will not misrepresent or embellish our relationship with you (including by expressing or implying that we support, sponsor, or endorse you)…

So: say we are Associates and we earn. Do **not** say Amazon endorses, sponsors, or partners with Letter Studio.

### 1.2 FTC / Amazon link-level disclosure

Amazon help “Why do I have to identify myself as an Associate?” (official):

> To meet the Associate Program's requirements, you must **(1)** include a legally compliant disclosure with your links **and (2)** identify yourself on your Site as an Amazon Associate with the language required by the Operating Agreement.

For (1), Amazon’s help restates FTC rules:

1. **Clear.** Amazon’s examples: `(paid link)`, `#ad`, or `#CommissionsEarned`.
2. **Conspicuous.** “It should be placed near any affiliate link or product review in a location that customers will notice easily. They shouldn't have to hunt for it.”

Amazon points Associates to the FTC FAQ (affiliate section) and Disclosures 101.

**FTC law the Guides interpret:** Section 5 of the FTC Act, 15 U.S.C. § 45 (unfair or deceptive acts). The Guides themselves are **not** a statute; they are the Commission’s interpretation of how § 5 applies (16 CFR § 255.0(a)). Practices inconsistent with the Guides can still draw an FTC action under § 5.

**16 CFR § 255.5(a)** (material connections):

> When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is not reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. … Material connections can include … monetary payment … A material connection needs to be disclosed when a significant minority of the audience for an endorsement does not understand or expect the connection. A disclosure of a material connection does not require the complete details of the connection, but it must clearly communicate the nature of the connection sufficiently for consumers to evaluate its significance.

**Affiliate-link example — 16 CFR § 255.5(b) Example 11:**

> A coffee lover creates a blog that reviews coffee makers. The blogger writes the content independently of the marketers of the coffee makers but includes affiliate links to websites on which consumers can buy these products from their marketers. Whenever a consumer clicks on such a link and buys the product, the blogger receives a portion of the sale. Because knowledge of this compensation could affect the weight or credibility site visitors give to the blogger's reviews, the reviews should clearly and conspicuously disclose the compensation.

Our “Send it yourself” links are the same kind of connection: we recommend kits / envelopes / stamps / paper and earn if the visitor buys on Amazon after clicking.

**What “clear and conspicuous” means — 16 CFR § 255.0(f):**

> “clear and conspicuous” means that a disclosure is difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers. … A visual disclosure, by its size, contrast, location, the length of time it appears, and other characteristics, should stand out from any accompanying text or other visual elements so that it is easily noticed, read, and understood. … In any communication using an interactive electronic medium, such as social media or the internet, the disclosure should be **unavoidable**. The disclosure should not be contradicted or mitigated by, or inconsistent with, anything else in the communication.

**FTC FAQ (affiliate section), as quoted on the official page and cited by Amazon:**

- Disclose the retailer relationship clearly and conspicuously so readers can decide how much weight to give the recommendation.
- “The closer the disclosure is to your recommendation, the better.”
- “**Paid link**” right next to an affiliate link “should be an adequate disclosure of the nature of the link.”
- “**Commissionable link**” is **not** a clear disclosure.

**FTC 16 CFR § 255.0(g) Example 9** (placement failures — do not do these):

- Disclosure only on a profile page / other page, while the paid post itself has none → not clear and conspicuous; people seeing the paid content can miss it.
- Endorsement visible without clicking “more,” disclosure only after “more” → not unavoidable.
- Tiny, low-contrast, competing text, short duration → easy to miss.

### 1.3 What `/disclosure` must say

Use the Amazon sentence as the first line. Then, in plain English, say we earn a commission if the visitor buys through our tagged Amazon links, at no extra cost to them. That is the FTC “nature of the connection.” Do not bury the Amazon sentence under a wall of terms.

Recommended live copy (already drafted in `src/legal.js`; keep the first sentence exact):

1. **Amazon** — `As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.`
2. **What that means** — Some links to envelopes, stamps, certified-mail kits, and paper go to Amazon.com with our Associates tag. If you buy after clicking, Amazon may pay us a commission. You pay the same price. We do not get your Amazon account data.
3. **What we do not do** — No cloaking, no Amazon links in unsolicited email, no Amazon logos as ours, no claim that Amazon endorses Letter Studio, no cookie-stuffing.
4. **Other programs** — List them here *before* those links go live.
5. **FTC** — This is a paid relationship.

`/disclosure` is extra explanation. It does **not** satisfy OA §5 or FTC § 255.5 by itself.

### 1.4 Privacy / cookies (Amazon Program Policies, not FTC endorsements)

Participation Requirements **§3(e)** requires the Site to disclose, via a privacy policy or otherwise, use of cookies / pixels / similar tech by you and third parties, and that third parties (including Amazon) may collect information and set cookies. Current `src/legal.js` privacy text covers Amazon cookies if the visitor clicks an affiliate link. Keep that true.

## 2. Placement rules (near the links, not only buried)

| Rule | Source | What it means for letterstudio.net |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon sentence “clearly and prominently” / “clearly and conspicuously” **on the Site** | OA §5; Amazon help GHQNZAU6669EZS98 | Put the **exact sentence** on every page that has Special Links, in the same block as the links. A footer-only or `/disclosure`-only placement is the classic fail. |
| FTC disclosure **near** the link or review; visitors must not hunt | Amazon help; FTC FAQ | Same aside, immediately before or beside the first link, not only after a long page of letter cards. |
| Disclosure **unavoidable** on the internet | 16 CFR § 255.0(f) | Visible without clicking “more,” expanding a `<details>`, scrolling into a tiny grey footer, or opening `/disclosure`. |
| Same **medium** as the recommendation | Amazon help | If we ever recommend a product in email, SMS, social, or voice, disclose in that same medium. Profile bio / footer / `/disclosure` alone is not enough (FTC Example 9). |
| Social: Amazon sentence “associated with your account” **plus** per-post FTC disclosure | Amazon help | Only if we post Special Links off-site. |
| Do not imply Amazon sponsorship | OA §5 | No “official partner,” no Amazon logo lockup with our mark. |

**Practical layout that matches both layers:**

```
Send it yourself
As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. (paid link)

• Certified mail kit — …
• Business envelopes — …
• Forever stamps — …

Full disclosure → /disclosure
```

The Amazon sentence **above** the list (same contrast as body text, not `.fine` grey) plus `(paid link)` next to the heading or each link is the conservative reading of Amazon’s two-layer help page + FTC “paid link next to the link.”

A group disclosure immediately **under** the list, in the same `<aside>`, is closer to “near” than a site footer — but muted “fine print” after the links is the weaker form. 16 CFR § 255.0(f) judges size, contrast, and location. Do not style the required sentence as an afterthought.

## 3. Trademark / logo rules

**We must not use the Amazon logo or Amazon Smile mark** unless Amazon has supplied a permitted mark and we follow the Trademark Guidelines exactly.

### 3.1 Program Policies — Trademark Guidelines (Apr 14, 2026)

Quoted rules:

1. Amazon Marks may be used **only** “BY DISPLAY ON YOUR SITE WITH THE PURPOSE OF ADVERTISING AVAILABILITY OF PRODUCTS ON AN AMAZON SITE, WITH A CORRESPONDING SPECIAL LINK TO THAT SITE.”
2. Use must comply with the Guidelines and all Program Documents.
3. Do **not** use Marks in a way that implies sponsorship or endorsement; do not disparage; do not damage goodwill; **do not use in offline material or email** (printed material, mailing, SMS, MMS, email attachment, oral solicitation).
4. “We will supply an image or images of the Amazon Marks for you to use.” Do not alter proportion, color, font, or elements.
5. Each Mark must stand alone with reasonable clear space; no busy/interfering background.
6. All rights and goodwill belong to Amazon.
7. Do not use a third-party seller’s logo next to a Special Link without that seller’s written authorization.
8. Do not use or apply to register any mark, domain, subdomain, username, or app name confusingly similar to an Amazon mark.

### 3.2 Standalone Trademark Guidelines page

Additional official points:

- Offline / printed / mailing use of Amazon Marks is forbidden.
- If a supplied Amazon Mark **is** displayed: “You must prominently include a statement on your site stating that Amazon and the Amazon logo are trademarks of Amazon.com, Inc. or its affiliates.” Example they give: `Amazon and the Amazon logo are trademarks of Amazon.com, Inc. or its affiliates.`
- Do not put Amazon Marks (or misspellings) in domain names, social usernames, or software names. `letterstudio.net` is fine.

### 3.3 What logo, if any, is allowed

From the official Trademark Guidelines page (“Available at Amazon Logo Use Guidelines”):

> The Amazon logo and the Amazon Smile mark are **not permitted** to be used by influencers or publishers because we provide the “available at Amazon” logo for use in advertising.

The **only** graphic Amazon currently offers Associates/publishers without a special business-development approval is the official **“available at Amazon”** lockup (white or squid ink; download from Amazon’s brand-usage zip). Rules if we ever use it:

- Official file only. Do not recreate, outline, distort, rotate, recolor, or add/remove elements.
- Clear space = height of the letters in the Amazon wordmark; scales with size.
- Minimum size (stacked): 0.75 in print / 90 px (1x) / 180 px (2x). Horizontal: 1.4 in / 140 px / 280 px.
- Do not put it on a low-contrast or busy photo.
- Small space: typeset “Available at Amazon” (capitalize “Available”) instead of shrinking the badge below minimum.
- If we display it, add the trademark attribution sentence in §3.2.

**Default for Letter Studio: use no Amazon logo at all.** Text links (“Certified mail kit”) are enough. Do not scrape the smile, the wordmark, or packaging art.

### 3.4 Paid search / Amazon words

Commission Income Statement §2(c) and Participation Requirements §6(e)–(f): do not bid on “amazon”, “kindle”, other Amazon Marks, or misspellings (`ammazon`, `amaozn`, `kindel`) as paid-search keywords if the ad sends the user to Amazon (a **Prohibited Paid Search Placement**). Organic or paid ads may send users to **our** Site, not directly or via a Redirecting Link to Amazon.

## 4. Prohibited: unsolicited email, cookie stuffing, cloaking (and related)

Amazon’s policies do not always use the street names. The binding text is below.

### 4.1 Unsolicited email / offline promotion with Amazon links

Participation Requirements **§4 Promotional Limitations**:

- No Promotional Activities on Amazon’s behalf except those the Agreement expressly permits.
- **No offline** use of Amazon Marks, Program Content, or Special Links — “printed material, ebook, mailing, or any oral solicitation.”
- Special Links **may** go in email, SMS, and social DMs **only if** “such communications are solicited (i.e., opted into by the receiving customer)” and otherwise comply with the Agreement, Trademark Guidelines, and Amazon Brand Usage Guidelines.
- For CAN-SPAM / TCPA, **we** are the “Sender.”
- Amazon can demand sample materials and a written certification of compliance.

**Rule for us:** never put `tag=openclam-20` (or any Special Link) in cold email, purchased lists, or any message the recipient did not opt into. Draft-delivery email (`src/mailer.js`) must stay a letter, not an Amazon flyer. CRM note in `business/crm/README.md` already forbids this.

### 4.2 Cloaking

Participation Requirements **§6(v)** (Amazon’s word):

> You will not **cloak**, hide, spoof, or otherwise obscure the URL of your Site containing Special Links (including by use of Redirecting Links) or the user agent of the application in which Program Content is displayed or used such that we cannot reasonably determine the site or application from which a customer clicks through such Special Link to an Amazon Site.

Also **§6(w):** do not use a link shortener / button / hyperlink in a way that makes it unclear the destination is an Amazon Site.

Also **§6(n):** do not frame Amazon in our Site or a WebView.

Also Commission Income Statement **§2(e)** — **Redirecting Link** (purchase disqualified): a link that sends the user to Amazon via an intermediate site **without** a click or other affirmative action on that intermediate site.

Official auto-redirect FAQ (GVFBVT6N73NTC933):

> No. Per the Operating Agreement, we do not allow Associates to automatically redirect visitors to Amazon.com or to cause the Amazon.com site to automatically open either within a frame or in a new browser window.

**Rule for us:** `href` goes to `https://www.amazon.com/…&tag=openclam-20`. Visitor clicks. No JS bounce, no meta-refresh, no hidden iframe, no cloaked shortener that hides amazon.com.

### 4.3 Cookie stuffing / fake sessions (official clauses)

Amazon’s Program Policies do not use the phrase “cookie stuffing.” These clauses prohibit it:

- **§6(q):** “You will not attempt to circumvent the Commission Income Statement or artificially increase your commission income. For example, you cannot cause any page of an Amazon Site to open in a customer’s browser other than as a result of the customer clicking on a Special Link on your Site.”
- **§6(s):** “You will not artificially generate clicks or impressions on your Site or create Sessions on an Amazon Site, whether by way of a robot or software program or otherwise.”
- **§6(r):** no intercepting / redirecting traffic or diverting commission from other Associates.
- **§2(e)** Redirecting Links (above).
- Commission Income Statement intro to §2: Qualifying Purchases are disqualified when they occur in connection with a Program Documents violation.

**Rule for us:** no pixels, 1×1 images, prefetch, or scripts that hit Amazon or drop an Associates cookie without a real user click on our Special Link.

### 4.4 Other Program Policy bans that apply to this Site

From Participation Requirements §6 and related sections (not invented; listed because they are easy to trip):

| Ban | Cite |

A page that is only a pile of Amazon search links would fail.
