These are Brand B, Stripe-prohibited, UPL, or both. `getSku()` returns null. Ads, guides, and blogs do not pitch them.

### Unauthorized practice of law (we are not a law firm)

Do not add these as SKUs or as “also we can…” in copy:

- Demand letters sold as legal demands
- Intent-to-sue / “we will sue”
- Cease-and-desist sold as an enforceable order
- Eviction / unlawful-detainer letters
- Immigration / visa / asylum letters
- Collections letters that threaten credit reporting or wage garnishment
- Anything “on attorney letterhead”
- Anything we mail, file, serve, or sign as counsel
- State-statute packs (SCRA, cooling-off, “your state requires…”) presented as advice

LegalZoom, Rocket Lawyer, LegalShield, LetterDash, and JusticeDirect do versions of this **because they are (or sit on) law firms**. We are not.

A **factual company complaint** the customer sends is allowed. A **threat packaged as law** is not.

## Claims we will not use

Do not put these in ads, landing pages, emails, Stripe descriptors, or letter footers.

### Legal / outcome

- “Legal advice,” “legal cancellation,” “legal protection,” “know your rights” as a product promise
- “Attorney-drafted,” “lawyer-approved,” “attorney letterhead,” “meet court requirements”
- “We will send it,” “we handle the rest,” “we file,” “we serve,” “we call them”
- “Stop paying,” “stop the charges,” “they have to honor this”
- “Guaranteed cancellation,” “guaranteed refund from them,” “they must reply”
- “Chargeback kit,” “if they keep charging we’ll…”
- “Intent to sue,” “or we will take legal action” as our voice (the customer can write their own letter; we do not stock that threat)
- “Peace of mind” tied to a legal result
- “Works in all 50 states” as a legal claim
- Citations to SCRA, FTC, state gym statutes, FDCPA, TCPA as if we are applying the law to their facts

Allowed, and already live: **“This is not legal advice. You send it yourself.”** Keep saying it.

### FTC / UDAAP / DoNotPay graveyard

- “World’s first robot lawyer”
- “AI lawyer,” “AI attorney,” “better than a lawyer,” “replace your lawyer”
- “Fights for you,” “beat bureaucracy,” “get what you deserve”
- “We’ve saved customers $X million”
- Fake before/after money saved
- “No cancellation hassle” as a legal outcome
- Testimonials we did not get, or dollar amounts we did not verify

FTC order to read before anyone gets cute:  
[https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/02/ftc-finalizes-order-donotpay-prohibits-deceptive-ai-lawyer-claims-imposes-monetary-relief-requires)

### Superlatives and borrowed stats

- “#1,” “America’s only 5-star,” “industry-leading”
- Their numbers: ToastWiz “70,000+,” Eulogy Expert “20,000+,” FuneralSpeech “1800+ families,” Grammarly “40 million”
- Star clusters with no source
- Press logos we did not earn (ToastWiz’s NYT/WSJ/Wired wall)

### Quality guarantees we cannot keep

- “Mistake-free,” “perfect,” “superior quality”
- “Will not contain errors” (Eulogy Expert admits AI invents facts — we do not hide that risk by promising the opposite)
- “Sounds exactly like you”
- “100% unique / not a template” as a metaphysical claim
- Default **100% money-back if you don’t like the vibe** after the draft is emailed (Terms: sale is final once drafted, except omitted required fields or failed delivery). Wide MBGs are how ByeGym/Eulogy Expert buy conversions and eat chargebacks.

### Pricing tricks

- “$199” when the real total is $199+$229 (LetterDash)
- Hidden service fees
- 7-day free trial that becomes $39.99/mo (Rocket Lawyer / LegalZoom pattern)
- “Was $99 now $19” fake markdowns
- Comparison charts that say we are “just as good as a $600 attorney letter”

### Brand / category confusion

- Calling ourselves a funeral home, therapist, HR department, or law firm
- “Tribute” as a Tribute.co clone (they sell video montages)
- “LegalZoom for letters”
- Naming ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini as the product (“powered by GPT-X”)
- Adult, timeshare, credit, or ID-theft ad groups on the same domain

## Quick test before shipping copy

1. Would this sentence still be true if the gym ignores the letter? If no, cut it.
2. Would a state bar read this as practicing law? If yes, cut it.
3. Would Stripe’s restricted-business list catch the SKU? If yes, do not build it.
4. Did a competitor get an FTC order for this claim? If yes, do not “tone it down.” Drop it.
5. Are we citing a review count or “#1” we did not measure? Cut it.

If in doubt, it goes in this file, not on the site.
